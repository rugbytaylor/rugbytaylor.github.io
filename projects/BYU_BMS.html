Custom Automotive Battery Management System

Formula Society of Automotive Engineers (FSAE)

Objective

Design and build a custom automotive-grade Battery Management System (BMS) for BYU Racing's FSAE electric formula car, capable of safely monitoring and balancing a 108S4P high-voltage accumulator built from 9 series-connected modules. As lead EE on a team of 4 (two of whom were freshmen/sophomores), I owned the technical direction of the project from schematic through PCB layout, while also managing timelines, setting stretch goals, and stepping in wherever the team needed extra hands to keep the project moving. This has been an ongoing project since late June 2026, with first-iteration hardware now manufactured and in active testing.

Key Requirements

  • 108S4P pack architecture, 9 modules in series, each module 12S4P
  • Per-cell-group voltage monitoring and passive balancing via LTC6811-1 per module
  • 12 thermistors per module (one per parallel cell stack) for full thermal coverage
  • Reliable isoSPI daisy-chain communication across all 9 modules
  • Automotive-grade component selection prioritizing reliability and serviceability
  • Custom flexible PCB (FPC) voltage tap connections between cells and slave boards

System Architecture

The system is split into slave boards and a central master board. Each of the 9 modules carries its own slave board built around an LTC6811-1 battery monitor IC, reading voltage across every 4P parallel cell group in its 12S4P module and driving passive balancing when needed. Each slave board also monitors 12 thermistors, one per parallel cell stack, giving full thermal visibility across every group in the module rather than a handful of spot checks. I fully owned the schematic capture and PCB layout for the slave boards, including component selection focused on automotive-grade reliability and ease of field service.

The master board ties all 9 modules together over an isoSPI daisy chain, aggregating pack-wide voltage and temperature data. It's built around an STM32 for processing and communication, with onboard indicator LEDs for quick system status at a glance, a USB port for direct debugging and data pull, and CAN adapters for integration with the rest of the car's electronics. I handled the PCB layout for the master board, working from the schematic the team developed together.

Design Challenges: Custom Flex PCBs for Voltage Sensing

One of the trickiest parts of the system was getting reliable, compact voltage tap connections from the busbars to each slave board without adding bulky wiring or unreliable connectors into a space-constrained module. I took this on solo, teaching myself flexible PCB (FPC) design from scratch, everything from stackup selection and bend radius constraints to coverlay, stiffeners, and connector footprints, and designed a custom FPC solution to carry voltage taps cleanly from the cells to the slave board.

The final design was polished enough that I presented it in a design review with an applications engineer from an FPC manufacturer, who gave positive feedback on the layout and manufacturability. Going from zero flex-PCB experience to a production-ready design that held up under outside engineering scrutiny was one of the most rewarding parts of this project, and it's a skill set that sets me apart from most of my peers.

Leadership

With a four-person team, I ended up driving most of the day-to-day direction of the project. That meant setting and adjusting timelines as manufacturing and testing realities came up, pushing the team toward stretch goals once core requirements were on track, and stepping in personally to pick up tasks whenever a teammate fell behind or hit a wall. Balancing hands-on design work with keeping the whole project on schedule was as much a part of this role as the electrical engineering itself.

Results

First-iteration slave and master boards have been manufactured and are currently in bench testing, with voltage and temperature readings confirmed across cell groups and balancing behavior under active validation. The custom FPC voltage taps have proven reliable during initial bring-up testing, and the modular slave/master architecture has made isolating and debugging individual modules straightforward. The team is continuing testing and iteration ahead of full pack integration.
